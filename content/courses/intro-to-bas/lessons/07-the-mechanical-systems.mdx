We've spent five lessons describing the BAS. This one steps back to the equipment underneath. A BAS is only as useful as its understanding of the mechanical system it sits on top of, and that mechanical system almost always falls into one of three families: **air-side**, **water-side**, and **refrigerant-side**.

The same building usually contains all three. A typical mid-rise office runs air-side equipment (air handlers and VAVs) connected to water-side equipment (a chiller plant and a heating plant), with refrigerant-side equipment sprinkled in for spaces the central plant doesn't serve. Knowing which side a problem belongs to is the first move when something goes wrong.

<LessonSection title="The AHU as the air-side/water-side handoff" level={2}>

<LessonSection title="Water-side: chillers, boilers, and the loops between them" level={2}>

Once you leave the air-side equipment, you enter pipes. A **central plant** is the family of machines that makes the working fluids the rest of the building uses, typically chilled water around 42–45°F supply, hot water around 140–180°F supply, and condenser water that connects <KeyTerm term="chillers" definition="Machines that remove heat from a stream of water. Water in warm, water out cold. The heat is rejected either to outside air (air-cooled) or to a cooling tower (water-cooled)." /> to <KeyTerm term="cooling towers" definition="Where the heat removed by a water-cooled chiller actually leaves the building. Warm condenser water cascades through fill, partially evaporates, and returns cooler." />.

<LessonSection title="Where the BAS plugs in" level={2}>

For every piece of equipment above, the BAS performs the same basic moves:

- **Read** sensors and state, temperatures, pressures, flow rates, on/off, alarms.
- **Compute** based on setpoints, schedules, and supervisory logic.
- **Command** outputs, valve and damper positions, fan and pump VFD speeds, equipment enable/disable.
- **Record** trends and alarms for later investigation.

The shape of the work changes, but the verbs don't. Once you can read a sequence of operations for an AHU, you can read one for a chiller plant, you're looking for the same five questions, just answered with different machines.

<Aside attribution="A senior commissioning agent, on what makes a good BAS engineer">
  The ones who get good fastest treat air-side, water-side, and refrigerant-side
  as dialects of the same language. The bad ones treat them as foreign
  countries.
</Aside>

</LessonSection>
