## A day in 5 timestamps

## Pressing play on an AHU

The first piece of equipment a BAS commands every morning is the air handler. AHUs don't just "turn on", they execute a startup sequence designed to protect themselves and the building.

<Callout variant="industry-insight">
  Most BAS bugs operators report are really sequence bugs: a step that didn't
  fire, fired in the wrong order, or fired without the upstream device proving
  its state. Knowing the *order* matters more than knowing the parts.
</Callout>

## Five ideas worth flipping

A handful of vocabulary terms power most of what we've seen so far. Tap each card to see the definition, and tap again to hide it.

<RevealCard
  front="Setpoint"
  back="The value the BAS is trying to reach for a controlled variable, e.g., 72°F zone temperature, or 1.5 inches w.c. duct static pressure."
/>

<RevealCard
  front="Setback"
  back="An unoccupied setpoint, looser than the occupied one to save energy. In heating season a zone might hold 72°F during the day and drift down to ~65°F overnight; in cooling season the corresponding move is a higher unoccupied setpoint (sometimes called setup), often in the high 70s or low 80s °F."
/>

<RevealCard
  front="Discharge-air temperature (DAT)"
  back="The temperature of air leaving the AHU. The control variable for most cooling-coil loops."
/>

<RevealCard
  front="Static pressure"
  back="The pressure inside a duct. Supply fans are usually commanded to a static-pressure setpoint that holds steady as VAVs open and close."
/>

<RevealCard
  front="Demand-control ventilation"
  back="Resetting the outside-air intake based on real-time occupancy signals, usually CO₂, while still meeting the code-required minimum ventilation rate and any pressurization constraints. Saves heating and cooling energy when spaces are lightly used, without dropping below what the ventilation standard demands."
/>
