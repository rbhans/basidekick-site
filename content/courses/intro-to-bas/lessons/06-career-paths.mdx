The last lesson covered the *people on a project*. This one covers the *people in the industry*, how they got there, what they do day-to-day, and how a career in BAS tends to evolve over a decade or two.

The short version: there is no single ladder. People arrive in BAS from HVAC service, from electrical work, from IT, from mechanical engineering, and from the operator's chair. The industry absorbs all of them, and the best engineers are usually the ones who brought a strong skill from somewhere else.

<LessonSection title="The roles people grow into" level={2}>

A few roles capture most professional BAS work. They aren't titles every company uses, different shops use the same words to mean different things, but the shapes are consistent. Two of them, the <KeyTerm term="controls technician" definition="The field-focused role: pulls cable, mounts and terminates controllers, walks down sequences. Stitches BAS, lighting, metering, security, and IT into a single supervisory layer. Often abbreviated MSI." />, sit at opposite ends of the field-to-systems spectrum and are worth recognizing by name.

<LessonSection title="Adjacent paths" level={2}>

A few specialties grow naturally out of BAS but aren't really BAS work anymore. They show up often enough in conversation that it helps to recognize them.

- **Energy management**, looking at meters, utility bills, and trend data to find waste. Lives downstream of the BAS but feeds requirements back into it.
- **Sustainability / decarbonization**, broader strategic work for owners, with BAS as one of several tools. Often paired with an engineering degree or LEED / WELL accreditations.
- **Cybersecurity**, increasingly visible as BAS networks touch corporate IT. Few people specialize fully in BAS cybersecurity yet; most hold a foot in OT cyber more broadly.
- **Vendor / manufacturer**, joining one of the BAS manufacturers (Honeywell, Siemens, JCI, Schneider, Distech, Tridium, ABB, etc.) as an applications engineer, trainer, or product manager.

<Callout variant="tip">
  If you're early in your career and want optionality, build one platform deep
  and one protocol broad. A practitioner who is excellent at, say, Niagara
  Tridium <em>and</em> understands BACnet/IP across vendors can choose where
  they work for decades.
</Callout>

</LessonSection>

<LessonSection title="How people learn" level={2}>

There is no single curriculum. Most career growth in BAS happens through three sources, in roughly this order:

1. **On-the-job apprenticeship.** Working alongside a senior technician or engineer for a year or two is more valuable than any course.
2. **Vendor training.** Tridium University, Honeywell EBI, JCI Metasys, Siemens, each major vendor runs structured training on their platform. Many companies pay for it.
3. **Industry organizations.** ASHRAE, ACG (commissioning), AEE (energy), and BACnet International all run conferences, certifications, and standards committees that anchor the broader field.
